Term 1 · Weeks 1–2

Welcome back & Positive Education intro

Weekly focuses: Welcome back · Positive Education intro

NO PREP — Week focus activity

Welcome back: Quick connection circle

  • Students answer: one thing I’m looking forward to, one thing that helps me settle, or one thing I want HG to feel like this year.
  • Build a shared list of “what makes a good Home Group”.
  • Finish with one class agreement: how we will start each HG positively.

NO PREP — Second week activity

Positive Education intro: What helps people thrive?

  • Put PERMAH on the board: Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment, Health.
  • Students choose one pillar and name one tiny action that supports it at school.
  • Link the action to the idea that wellbeing can be practised, not just talked about.

Accessibility

All activities can be run verbally. Students can respond silently, with a partner, by thumbs/fingers, or by opting out of personal sharing.

Term 1 · Weeks 3–4

PERMAH overview & Virtues & character strengths

Weekly focuses: PERMAH overview · Virtues & character strengths

NO PREP — Week focus activity

PERMAH snap examples

  • Call out everyday school moments: laughing with a friend, finishing homework, helping someone, joining sport, sleeping well.
  • Students point to or say which PERMAH pillar it fits.
  • Emphasise that small daily choices build wellbeing.

NO PREP — Second week activity

Strength spotting

  • Students think of someone in HG who used a strength this week.
  • Share examples without embarrassing anyone: “I saw kindness when…” or “I saw perseverance when…”.
  • Explain that character strengths are habits we can notice, practise and grow.

Accessibility

All activities can be run verbally. Students can respond silently, with a partner, by thumbs/fingers, or by opting out of personal sharing.

Term 1 · Weeks 5–6

Virtue of Humanity & Love

Character strengths: Humanity · Love

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Gotcha!GGS Brain Breaks #2, p.17

p.17

Students stand in a circle. Left hand out flat, palm up. Right index finger into the left palm of the person to your right. On 'Go!' — grab the finger to your left while escaping the grab on your right.

Say it like this: 'Left hand flat, right finger in someone else's palm. When I say go — grab left, escape right. Ready?'

Why this lesson: Physical connection with others — an embodied warm-up for Love & Kindness.

Accessibility

  • Students who prefer not to make physical contact can hold the timer or count grabs instead.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Brené Brown on Empathy — youtu.be/1Evwgu369Jw (2:53). Pairs discuss: what's the difference between empathy and sympathy?

1.Play the clip (2:53). Pairs: when did someone go into the dark with you rather than shine a light from outside? 60 seconds each.

2.The Love strength is choosing to go in. The Kindness strength is doing it even when it's uncomfortable.

► Empathy means going into the hard feeling with someone — not trying to make it go away. That's Love and Kindness in action.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Ask: 'Think of a time someone made you feel worse by trying to make you feel better. What would have actually helped?'

Accessibility

  • Students can describe a character from a book or show rather than sharing personally.

Term 1 · Weeks 7–8

Social Intelligence & Kindness

Character strengths: Social Intelligence · Kindness

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Look Down, Look Up!GGS Brain Breaks #4, p.19⚠ Small object (place on floor)

p.19

Students stand in a circle. Stare at the object on the floor. Each student secretly chooses someone to look at on 'Look Up!' — if that person has also chosen you, both step out.

Say it like this: 'Everyone stare at the object on the floor. In your head, pick one person you'll look at when I say Look Up. If they picked you too — you're both out. Don't tell anyone who you picked.'

Why this lesson: Literally practises reading others' choices — a direct warm-up for Social Intelligence.

Accessibility

  • Students who find sustained eye contact distressing can step out when tapped by the teacher rather than via eye contact.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Groups of 3. Each person independently writes one word for how the person in the scenario feels — then compare without discussing first.

1.Scenario: 'Someone gets their test back, looks at it, closes it face down, and doesn't say anything.' Each person writes privately. Then share and compare.

2.How different are your reads? Social Intelligence means staying curious rather than certain about how someone feels.

► Reading how someone feels is a skill — and we all do it differently. The Social Intelligence strength is asking: could I be wrong about this person right now?

If this doesn't work: Won't write? List four possible feelings on the board and ask the class to vote — same debrief.

Accessibility

  • Extend writing time for students who need it.
  • EALD students can write in any language and point to key words when sharing.

Term 1 · Weeks 9–10

Virtue of Transcendence & Humour

Character strengths: Transcendence · Humour

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Air PerformancesGGS Brain Breaks #13, p.30

p.30

Call out an imaginary activity — air guitar, air skipping, air tennis, air juggling, air piano. Students perform their best virtual version with full commitment.

Say it like this: 'Everyone stand up. I'll call an activity — you perform your best version without any equipment. Full commitment. Here we go — air guitar!'

Why this lesson: High energy, absurdist, zero stakes — a direct warm-up for Humour & Awe.

Accessibility

  • Students who find this overstimulating or physically difficult can be the director — they call out which air activity the class does next.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Kid President — How to Change the World — youtu.be/4z7gDsSKUmU (3:30). What's the serious point behind the humour?

1.Play the clip (3:30). Pairs: 'Where did you laugh — and what was the serious point behind that moment?' 60 seconds.

2.The Humour strength does something argument can't — it lowers the guard. What would be harder to say without humour?

► Humour isn't trivial — it changes the temperature of a room and gets serious things said. That's a real strength.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Ask: 'When did you last use humour to make a difficult moment easier? What made it work rather than backfire?'

Accessibility

  • Before the video, check: 'Has anything ever made you stop and just stare — something so big or strange you forgot what you were thinking?
  • That's awe.' Grounds the concept for younger or lower-literacy students.

Term 2 · Weeks 1–2

Spirituality / Meaning & Appreciation of Beauty

Character strengths: Spirituality / Meaning · Appreciation of Beauty

NO PREP — Open cold

Pairs. Ask: 'What would you keep doing even if no one was watching, marking, or rewarding it?' Interviewer asks one genuine follow-up. 90 seconds each.

1.Person A answers. Person B asks one follow-up — not a clarification, a real question that goes deeper. Swap.

2.What did you learn about your partner that you didn't know? That answer — that's their WHY.

► Purpose is personal. Finding out what drives the person next to you is how you start to actually know them.

Accessibility

  • Students who prefer not to speak off the cuff can write their answer first and read it to their partner.

Term 2 · Weeks 5–6

Virtue of Wisdom & Love of Learning

Character strengths: Wisdom · Love of Learning

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Find the FantaleGGS Brain Breaks #7, p.22⚠ 1 Fantale (hide before class)

p.22

Before class, hide a Fantale in the room. At the start of the lesson, call 'Find the Fantale!' Students hunt in a controlled way.

Say it like this: 'Before class I hid something in this room. You have one minute to find it — walk, don't run, don't move furniture. Go!'

Why this lesson: Curiosity activated physically — surprise and discovery before a Curiosity lesson.

Accessibility

  • Students with mobility restrictions direct others verbally — warmer or colder.
  • Check for food allergies before class; the treat can be swapped for any small object.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Groups of 3. Each person shares one thing they've genuinely wondered about recently — not a Google question, something they sat with. Group picks the most interesting one and discusses for 2 minutes.

1.60 seconds silent thinking — one genuine question each. Share within group. Choose the one that sparks most interest. Discuss for 2 minutes.

2.What would it take to follow one of those threads further?

► The Curiosity strength is activated by following something you actually wonder about — and finding out what the people around you wonder about.

If this doesn't work: No one will share? Teacher offers two genuine questions of their own first.

Accessibility

  • EALD students can write their question in any language and point to key words.
  • Reassure students: 'It can be anything — science, people, how something works.
  • Doesn't have to be deep.'

Term 2 · Weeks 7–8

Perspective & Curiosity

Character strengths: Perspective · Curiosity

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

What Do You Look Like When…?GGS Brain Breaks #9, p.24

p.24

Pairs stand back-to-back. One calls 'What do you look like when…?' Both prepare their face in secret, then spin 180° and reveal simultaneously.

Say it like this: 'Pairs, back to back. One of you calls a situation — start with: what do you look like when you find out school is cancelled? Two seconds, prepare your face. Then spin around and show each other at the same time.'

Why this lesson: Perspective-taking through expression — different people, same prompt, completely different reads.

Accessibility

  • Students with vestibular sensitivity or mobility restrictions can turn their head on 'reveal' rather than spinning.
  • Students who find facial expression difficult can write the emotion word instead.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Danger of a Single Story — TED-Ed animated — youtu.be/D9Ihs241zeg (3:30). One thing that stayed with you.

1.Play the clip (3:30). Rapid-fire around the room — one thing each. No repeating what someone already said.

2.Where in your life do you have a single story about someone or something? The Perspective strength is noticing it.

► Perspective means asking: what am I missing? Where do I only have one version of something? That's not a comfortable question — but it's the right one.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? 'A single story is when you only know one thing about a person or place — and that one thing becomes the whole story. Where do you have one?'

Accessibility

  • Pause the clip at 1:30 and check comprehension: 'What's the single story she's describing here?
  • Can anyone say it in one sentence?' before continuing.

Term 2 · Weeks 9–10

Creativity & Judgement

Character strengths: Creativity · Judgement

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Seven-11GGS Brain Breaks #8, p.23

p.23

Groups of 3. On 'Show!' all throw out 0–5 fingers on one hand. Goal: total exactly 7 — without talking. Try for 11 using two hands.

Say it like this: 'Groups of three. On my count — one, two, three, show — everyone throws out some fingers. Zero to five. Total exactly seven. No talking. Just fingers. Go.'

Why this lesson: Silent coordination and creative problem-solving — embodied Judgement & Creativity.

Accessibility

  • Students who find this frustrating can use a simplified version — two people, target of 5, unlimited attempts.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Groups of 3. You have 3 minutes to find the most creative solution to this problem — but it has to actually work.

1.Problem: 'Your school has 10 minutes less per day. Students are unhappy. How do you use those 10 minutes to genuinely improve school life?' Groups agree on one solution. Class votes: most creative AND most realistic.

2.Creativity isn't just novelty — it's novelty that works. Judgement is knowing the difference.

► Creativity means coming up with something new. Judgement means knowing if it actually works. A wild idea with no judgement is noise. Good judgement with no creativity just repeats what exists.

If this doesn't work: Problem too big? Simplify: 'Your home group has one free lesson per fortnight. What's the best use of it?'

Accessibility

  • Students who struggle to think aloud in groups can write their solution first, then advocate for it in discussion.

Term 3 · Weeks 1–2

Virtues recap & PERMAH recap

Weekly focuses: Virtues recap · PERMAH recap

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Pop-Up FactsGGS Brain Breaks #14, p.32

p.32

Students sit in a circle. Teacher calls a category and an action: 'Stand up if you have a sibling — and click your fingers.' Those who fit pop up, do the action, sit back down.

Say it like this: 'I'll call a category and an action. If it applies to you — stand up, do the action, sit back down. Ready: stand up if you have a sibling — and click your fingers.'

Why this lesson: Visibly reveals difference within the group — sets up the equity discussion naturally.

Accessibility

  • Check categories before class — avoid anything that could expose sensitive circumstances such as homelessness, family structure, or disability.
  • Safe defaults: moved house more than twice, have a pet, been to another country, play a sport.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Equity vs Equality animated — youtu.be/fvtqv5dyN-k (2:30). Pairs: one real example of equality-that-isn't-fair at this school, and one change that would make it equitable.

1.Play the clip (2:30). Pairs: one example + one realistic equitable change. 2 minutes. Pairs share.

2.The Fairness and Justice strengths require seeing the starting point, not just the finishing line.

► Equality means everyone gets the same thing. Equity means everyone gets what they actually need. Justice is noticing the difference — before someone has to point it out.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Teacher describes the fence image verbally. Pairs discuss from there.

Accessibility

  • Students who find the school example exposing can describe a fictional school instead.
  • Pause after the first image in the clip to check: 'What's the difference between what each person got?' before continuing.

Term 3 · Weeks 3–4

Virtue of Justice & Leadership

Character strengths: Justice · Leadership

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Line-Up (and Fold Around)GGS Brain Breaks #16, p.36

p.36

Students arrange themselves in one silent line by birthday. No talking — gestures only. Once lined up, the head folds around to meet the tail, creating random pairs for the lesson.

Say it like this: 'No talking from now until you're in the line. Arrange yourselves in order of birthday — January at that end, December at the other. No words. Go.'

Why this lesson: Self-organising without a nominated leader — leadership emerging from the group.

Accessibility

  • EALD students unsure of English month order can hold up fingers for the month number.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Rita Pierson — Every Kid Needs a Champion — youtu.be/SFnMTHhKdkw (0:00–3:30). Name one person who championed you and what they did.

1.Play 0:00–3:30. Pairs: each person names one person who championed them — specifically. What did they do? 60 seconds each.

2.The Leadership strength includes being a champion. The Citizenship strength is doing it for people who aren't your responsibility.

► You don't need a title to lead. You don't need permission to champion someone. The Leadership and Citizenship strengths are about choosing to act — for people who aren't obviously your responsibility.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Ask: 'Think of an adult who made you feel like you could do something you didn't think you could. What specifically did they do?'

Term 3 · Weeks 5–6

Fairness & Teamwork

Character strengths: Fairness · Teamwork

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Handstacking RacesGGS Brain Breaks #20, p.43

p.43

Teams of 6 form a circle, stack right hands then left hands on top. The bottom hand moves to the top — race to complete laps. Mark the top hand to signal the end of each lap.

Say it like this: 'Groups of six in a circle. Everyone put your right hand in — stack them. Now your left hand on top. The bottom hand moves to the top, one at a time. Practice. Now race.'

Why this lesson: Coordination, speed, and fairness under competition — perfect entry for Fairness & Teamwork.

Accessibility

  • Students with upper limb disability call the start and stop of each lap, or count completed rounds — integral to the race, not an observer.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Capuchin Monkeys — Unequal Pay — youtu.be/meiU6TxysCg (2:30). What does this tell us about where fairness comes from?

1.Play the clip (2:30). Pairs: 'Is the angry monkey right to be angry — or is it just reacting to difference? What's the actual injustice?' 60 seconds.

2.Fairness is instinct. The Fairness strength is what you do with that instinct — especially when the unfairness isn't directed at you.

► Fairness is instinct — you feel it before you can explain it. The strength is what you choose to do with that feeling, especially when it's not happening to you.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Describe the experiment: two monkeys, same task, one gets a grape, one a cucumber. The cucumber monkey throws it back. Same debrief.

Accessibility

  • Prime students before playing: 'You're going to watch two monkeys doing the same job for different pay.
  • Watch the one on the right.' The clip moves quickly and context helps.
  • Debrief can use a fictional frame — students don't need to name personal experiences of unfairness.

Term 3 · Weeks 9–10

Bravery & Perseverance

Character strengths: Bravery · Perseverance

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Eraser RaceGGS Brain Breaks #15, p.34⚠ 2 whiteboard erasers

p.34

Two students are nominated — one is the Racer, one is the Chaser. Both balance an eraser on their head. Racer gets a head start, then the walking race is on. If the eraser falls, stop and replace it.

Say it like this: 'Two volunteers. Balance an eraser on your head. One of you is the Racer, one is the Chaser. Racer gets a three-second head start. If the eraser falls — stop, replace it, keep going. Walking only.'

Why this lesson: Small physical risk, persistence when it falls — embodied Bravery & Perseverance.

Accessibility

  • Students with balance difficulties compete in a seated version — eraser on the knee, shuffling or wheeling.
  • Any soft flat object works if erasers aren't available.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: What Is Courage? — School of Life — youtu.be/H14bBuluwB8 (3:30). Courage is acting despite fear — not without it.

1.Play the clip (3:30). Groups of 3: each person names one thing they've kept going with even when it was hard — not finished, just kept going.

2.That's the Perseverance strength. What made you stay?

► Bravery is acting while the fear is still there. Perseverance is choosing not to stop. Both are decisions — not personality traits.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Ask: 'What's the difference between being fearless and being brave?' Take 2–3 responses, then go to groups.

Accessibility

  • 'Kept going with something hard' can feel exposing.
  • Offer: 'It can be something small — a game level, a habit, a friendship you worked at.
  • Doesn't have to be impressive.'

Term 4 · Weeks 1–2

Honesty & Courage review

Character strengths: Honesty · Courage review

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Whole-Body Scissors, Paper, RockGGS Brain Breaks #17, p.38

p.38

Full-body version. Scissors: two arms moving. Paper: body stretched straight, arms raised. Rock: scrunch into a ball. Pairs count in and commit fully.

Say it like this: 'You know Scissors Paper Rock — this time your whole body is the shape. Scissors: move both arms. Paper: stretch as straight as you can, arms up. Rock: scrunch into the smallest ball you can. Pairs — scissors, paper, rock, go.'

Why this lesson: Zest requires full commitment and no holding back — this makes that physical.

Accessibility

  • Students with physical restrictions use hand signals only — the standard version — or a seated equivalent: lean forward for rock, stretch arms for paper, move arms for scissors.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Groups of 3. Three statements — for each one decide: honest, kind, both, or neither? You must all agree before moving on.

1.Statements: 1. 'Your presentation was fine.' (when it wasn't) 2. 'I don't think that will work — here's why.' 3. 'I don't want to say anything that might hurt your feelings.' Discuss each.

2.Zest is the energy that makes honest conversations feel like investment rather than criticism.

► Honesty without care is just being blunt. Zest without honesty is just enthusiasm with no substance. Together — that's telling the truth in a way that actually lands.

If this doesn't work: Groups won't agree? 'Where did you disagree — and what does that reveal about what you each value?'

Accessibility

  • Students who need processing time write their individual answer first — honest, kind, both, or neither — before the group discusses.

Term 4 · Weeks 3–4

Virtue of Temperance & Prudence

Character strengths: Temperance · Prudence

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Boi-ing 7GGS Brain Breaks #6, p.21

p.21

Standing circle. Count up from 1. Every number containing 7 or divisible by 7, say 'boi-ing' instead. If someone gets it wrong, restart.

Say it like this: 'Standing circle. We count up from one. Every time you hit a number with a 7 in it — or a multiple of 7 — say boi-ing instead. So: one, two, three, four, five, six, boi-ing, eight. If two people speak at once or someone says the wrong thing, we restart at one.'

Why this lesson: Literally practises inhibitory control — the cognitive core of Self-Regulation.

Accessibility

  • Students with dyscalculia or number-processing difficulties can be the restart caller — when an error happens they call restart.
  • Active role, no number pressure.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: The Marshmallow Test animated — youtu.be/QX_oy9614HQ (3 min). The finding isn't about marshmallows — what's it actually about?

1.Play the clip (3 min). Pairs: 'What strategy did the children who waited use? Name the actual technique — not that they were patient, what did they do?' 60 seconds.

2.Self-Regulation isn't willpower — it's strategy. Prudence means thinking ahead far enough to use the right strategy before you need it.

► Self-Regulation is a skill, not a personality trait. Prudence means planning for the moment when you'll need it — before you're already in it.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Describe the experiment briefly: kids, marshmallow, 15-minute wait for two. Ask: 'What strategies would actually work?' Groups brainstorm.

Accessibility

  • Before playing, prime students: 'Watch for what the kids who waited actually do — not their faces, their actions.
  • What strategy do they use?'

Term 4 · Weeks 7–8

Forgiveness & Year in review

Character strengths: Forgiveness · Year in review

NO PREP — GGS Brain Break

Rain CircleGGS Brain Breaks #24, p.48

p.48

Students stand in a circle. The leader begins rubbing hands together — this passes around the circle like a wave. Then finger clicking, thigh slapping, foot stamping. Build the storm, then reverse back to silence.

Say it like this: 'Everyone stand in a circle. Watch me and copy — pass it to the right. Start with hands rubbing together. Pass it around. When it comes back to me, I'll add the next sound.'

Why this lesson: A collective, calming closing ritual — the ideal opener for a Year Review and Forgiveness of Self lesson.

Accessibility

  • Students with hearing impairment can follow the visual — seat them where they can see the teacher or a peer clearly.
  • The movement alone is enough to participate fully.

LOW PREP — Read ahead

Play: Kristin Neff — Self-Compassion animated — youtu.be/11U0h0DPu7k (3:30). We treat ourselves worse than strangers — why?

1.Play the clip (3:30). Pairs: 'Think of something you've been hard on yourself about. If a close friend said they'd done the exact same thing, what would you say to them?' 60 seconds each.

2.That gap — between what you'd say to them and what you say to yourself — is where the Forgiveness of Self strength lives.

► You would forgive a close friend immediately. The Forgiveness strength means applying that same response to yourself — not to excuse what happened, but to stop carrying it into next year.

If this doesn't work: Video won't load? Ask: 'Why is it easier to forgive other people than to forgive yourself?'

Accessibility

  • The debrief is genuinely sensitive.
  • Reassure students: 'It can be something small, or something from earlier this year.
  • You don't have to share anything serious.' Pause the clip at 1:00: 'She's saying we treat ourselves worse than a stranger.
  • Do you agree?'

Why this lesson: Forgiveness is about choosing to put something down. This activity creates a quiet moment to notice what students might still be carrying.
